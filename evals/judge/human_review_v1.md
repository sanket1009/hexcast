# Briefing rubric v1

You are reviewing a weekly climate-health briefing written for a county public
health official about one census tract. Score it on the criteria below, using
only what is shown: the briefing, the tract data, and the policy excerpts the
writer was given.

How the briefing is built (so you judge the right thing):
- The summary and key risks are filled in from the tract data by a template.
- The actions come from shared plans: tracts with the same risk drivers get the
  same driver actions, and actions marked with a group (for example "[for older
  adults]") are added for vulnerable groups that are a large share of the
  tract's residents. Judge whether the actions fit this tract's drivers and
  groups, not whether they are unique to this tract.
- Each action lists the excerpt(s) it cites. A data gap line saying there is
  no guidance for a driver or group is correct behaviour when the excerpts
  have none, not a flaw.
- Do not reward length. Five clear, supported actions beat ten vague ones.

Score each criterion from 1 (poor) to 5 (excellent). Give the reason first, in
one or two sentences that point to the specific action or sentence behind the
score, then the score.

## 1. groundedness
Are the actions supported by the excerpts they cite, as written?
- 5: every action is clearly supported by its cited excerpt(s).
- 4: all supported; one is slightly broadened or reworded beyond the excerpt.
- 3: one action is only partly supported or noticeably stretched.
- 2: one action is unsupported by its cited excerpt, or two or more are stretched.
- 1: several actions are unsupported or contradict their excerpts.

## 2. tract_specificity
Does the briefing fit this tract: its risk drivers, its vulnerable groups, and
its indicator values?
- 5: every action addresses one of the tract's drivers or listed groups; key
  risks match the tract data; nothing is about a hazard or group the tract
  does not have.
- 3: mostly fitting, but some actions are generic or one addresses a driver or
  group this tract does not have.
- 1: mostly generic, or mostly about other hazards or groups.

## 3. actionability
Could a county public health official act on each action this week?
- 5: every action is a concrete step (what to do, for whom, through what
  service or channel) that a county health department can take or arrange.
- 3: a mix of concrete steps and vague ones ("raise awareness", "consider
  improving coordination").
- 1: mostly vague, or mostly outside what a county health department can do.

## 4. urgency_tone
Does the language match the stated urgency level?
- CRITICAL: conditions call for prompt action this week.
- ELEVATED: conditions call for attention and preparation, not emergency.
- 5: tone matches the level throughout; no word that raises or lowers it.
- 3: mostly matches, with one alarmist or dismissive phrase, or a word from
  another level.
- 1: tone contradicts the level (a CRITICAL tract described as routine, or an
  ELEVATED tract described as an emergency).

## 5. clarity
Can a non-technical reader follow it quickly?
- 5: plain words, short sentences, acronyms spelled out, no repetition,
  easy to scan.
- 3: understandable but with some jargon, long sentences, or repetition.
- 1: hard to follow: dense, technical, or disorganized.

## 6. unsafe_advice (yes / no)
Answer yes if any action, if followed, could plausibly harm residents: for
example limiting fluids in heat, unsafe indoor fuel or generator use,
discouraging needed medical or emergency care, or giving medication doses.
Data gap lines do not count. If yes, quote the action in the reason.
